Vaccinations for Dogs and Cats

Vaccines protect your pet against serious, sometimes fatal diseases, and many dog parks, groomers and kennels require them. We build a vaccine plan around your pet's age and lifestyle.

At a glance

Protection for every stage of life

Like people, dogs and cats should be vaccinated against dangerous diseases. For most pets, vaccines are a regular part of their wellness exams throughout life, with the most important series given in the first months and years (see puppy and kitten care).

Vaccines protect your pet's health, and they are also required for admission to many dog parks, kennels and other public areas. If your pet is behind on vaccinations, our veterinarian can identify the right plan.

Core for dogs
Rabies, distemper, parvovirus and hepatitis (adenovirus)
Core for cats

Core and non-core vaccines

Which vaccines does my pet need?

Core vaccines are recommended for every pet. Non-core, or lifestyle, vaccines depend on where your pet goes and who it meets.

Why vaccinate

Three good reasons to stay up to date

  • Your pet's health

    Diseases such as parvovirus, distemper and panleukopenia are serious and often fatal, but preventable.

  • Access to the places you go

    Dog parks, groomers, daycare and boarding kennels usually ask for proof of vaccination.

  • Your family and community

    Rabies can spread to people. Vaccinating pets protects everyone around them.

Questions

Vaccination FAQ

Still have questions? Call (310) 310-3704.

Which vaccines does my dog need?

Every dog should have the core vaccines against rabies, distemper, parvovirus and hepatitis. Dogs that go to dog parks, groomers, daycare or boarding should also have the Bordetella (kennel cough) vaccine.

Which vaccines does my cat need?

Every cat should have the core vaccines against rabies, feline distemper (panleukopenia), calicivirus and rhinotracheitis. Cats that go outside, even occasionally, should also have the FeLV (feline leukemia) vaccine.

What is the puppy vaccine schedule?

Puppies get a first core vaccine at 6 to 8 weeks old and a booster every 3 to 4 weeks until they are 16 weeks old or older, then a booster at about 6 to 12 months. Your veterinarian sets the exact dates and adds rabies and any lifestyle vaccines.

Puppy and kitten care →

What is the kitten vaccine schedule?

Kittens get a first core vaccine at 6 to 8 weeks old and boosters every 3 to 4 weeks until they are 16 weeks old or older, then a booster at about one year. FeLV vaccination can start at 8 weeks for kittens at risk. Rabies follows the vaccine label and local law.

Puppy and kitten care →

How often do adult dogs and cats need vaccine boosters?

Adult core vaccines such as distemper and parvovirus are generally given every three years rather than every year. Rabies follows the vaccine label and California law, and lifestyle vaccines such as Bordetella and FeLV depend on your pet's risk. Your veterinarian will set your pet's schedule.

Does my indoor cat really need vaccines?

Yes. Core vaccines are recommended for all pets, even those that spend little or no time outdoors, because some of these viruses can be carried into the home.

Is the rabies vaccine required in California?

Yes. California law requires dogs to be vaccinated against rabies, and we recommend the rabies vaccine for cats as well. Rabies is fatal and can spread to people, so keeping pets vaccinated protects your family.

Can my pet have side effects after a vaccine?

Mild tiredness, a sore spot or a smaller appetite for a day or so can happen. Facial swelling, hives, repeated vomiting or trouble breathing need attention right away: call (310) 310-3704 during business hours, or the 24-hour emergency hospital after hours.
