Minimally Invasive Surgery for Dogs and Cats

Keyhole surgery replaces one large incision with two tiny ones and a camera. Patients feel less pain, need less anesthesia and usually return to normal activity in 5 to 7 days instead of about 14.

The future of veterinary medicine

Small incisions, big difference

New technology has made remarkable surgical techniques possible, and they are the future of veterinary medicine. Minimally invasive, or keyhole, procedures use two small incisions: a camera goes through one, so we avoid the large, painful incision of traditional surgery. We use them for laparoscopic spay, gastropexy, cryptorchid neuter and biopsy; for open abdominal, head and neck or chest procedures, see our soft tissue surgery.

With the camera, Dr. Pedraza can examine the liver, spleen, bladder, kidneys and small intestines to make sure every organ looks healthy. Activity restriction is usually only needed for the first 3 to 5 days.

Benefits

Why keyhole surgery is gentler

  • Much smaller incisions

    Two holes of about 1/4 inch instead of a 5 to 10 cm incision for a traditional spay.

  • Less pain

    Precise cuts mean up to 65% less post-operative pain after a laparoscopic spay.

  • Faster recovery

    Most patients are back to normal activity in 5 to 7 days instead of about 14.

  • Less anesthesia

    Procedures are quicker, so your pet spends less time under anesthesia.

  • Lower risk of infection

    Small incisions heal quickly and reduce the chance of infection.

  • A clear view inside

    The camera gives an excellent, magnified view of the internal organs.

Each laparoscopic procedure explained

Example: gastropexy

From a 10 to 12 inch incision to a few small ones

A preventive gastropexy used to require a 10 to 12 inch abdominal incision. Laparoscopically it is done through small incisions of at most 2 inches, with less scarring and bruising, less pain and a quicker, better recovery, and it can be combined with a laparoscopic spay or cryptorchid neuter.

  • Prevents the life-threatening twisting of the stomach
  • Avoids the cost and stress of treating bloat
  • Recommended for high-risk large breeds

Questions

Minimally invasive surgery FAQ

Still have questions? Call (310) 310-3704.

What is minimally invasive surgery for pets?

Also called keyhole surgery, it uses a camera and instruments inserted through two small incisions instead of one large one, which means less pain and a faster recovery.

Which procedures can be done this way?

Laparoscopic spay, cryptorchid neuter, gastropexy and biopsy, plus minimally invasive diagnostics such as gastrointestinal endoscopy, rhinoscopy, otoscopy and cystoscopy.

How long is recovery after minimally invasive surgery?

Most patients return to normal activity in 5 to 7 days, compared with about 14 days after traditional surgery, with activity restriction for the first 3 to 5 days.

Will my pet need stitches removed?

In most cases no suture removal is needed. After a gastropexy, sutures are removed in about 14 days.

How do I know if my pet is a candidate?

Is keyhole surgery the same as laparoscopy?

Laparoscopy is the keyhole technique used inside the abdomen: a camera and instruments pass through small incisions. At Sevilla Veterinary Hospital it covers spay, cryptorchid neuter, gastropexy and biopsy. Endoscopy is a related camera technique used mainly for diagnosis.

Laparoscopy in detail →

What is endoscopy and when is it used?

Endoscopy uses a small camera to look inside the stomach and intestines, nose, ears or bladder without open surgery, to help reach a diagnosis. We offer gastrointestinal endoscopy, rhinoscopy, otoscopy and cystoscopy. Dr. Pedraza will tell you if your pet's signs call for it.

Does my pet still need general anesthesia?

Yes. Keyhole procedures are done under general anesthesia with monitoring throughout, after a physical exam and pre-anesthetic bloodwork. Because the procedures are quicker, your pet usually spends less time under anesthesia than with traditional surgery.

How does laparoscopic gastropexy compare with open surgery?

An open gastropexy used to need a 10 to 12 inch incision, while the laparoscopic approach uses small incisions of at most 2 inches. Veterinary surgical guidance reports less pain and a shorter recovery than open surgery. Both aim to prevent the stomach from twisting.

Are keyhole procedures available for cats as well as dogs?

Yes, minimally invasive procedures are available for dogs and cats. Which one suits your pet depends on its size, its health and the problem we are treating, and Dr. Pedraza explains the options at the consultation.
