Physical Therapy and Rehabilitation for Dogs

Physical therapy helps dogs recover from orthopedic surgery and injuries. It restores mobility, reduces pain and rebuilds muscle strength for a quicker, more complete recovery.

At a glance

Recovery that goes further

Physical therapy plays a crucial role in the recovery and overall well-being of dogs, especially after orthopedic surgery or an injury. Each program is tailored to the needs of the individual dog.

Our physical therapy team works closely with Dr. Pedraza to create a complete rehabilitation plan for every patient, so your dog regains function and enjoys a better quality of life.

Most patients come to us after TPLO cruciate ligament surgery or other orthopedic surgery, and older dogs with stiff joints can also benefit as part of senior pet care.

Techniques

How we help your dog recover

  • 01

    Laser therapy

    Low-level laser to reduce pain and inflammation and accelerate tissue repair.

  • 02

    Massage therapy

    Promotes circulation, reduces muscle tension and helps your dog relax.

  • 03

    Therapeutic exercises

    Customized exercises that improve strength, flexibility and balance.

  • 04

    Electrical stimulation

    Gentle electrical currents that stimulate muscle contraction and improve muscle tone.

  1. 1

Who it helps

Dogs that can benefit from rehabilitation

Your veterinarian decides whether and when physical therapy is right for your dog.

After surgery

  • Cruciate ligament repair (TPLO)
  • Fracture repair
  • Patellar luxation surgery
  • Hip and other joint surgery

Injury and age

  • Sprains, strains and soft tissue injuries
  • Stiffness and arthritis in older dogs
  • Loss of muscle after time off the leg

After orthopedic surgery

Part of the recovery after TPLO and other surgery

After orthopedic procedures such as cruciate ligament surgery (TPLO) or fracture repair, physical therapy can help your dog use the leg sooner, rebuild muscle and return to normal activity safely.

Questions

Physical therapy FAQ

Still have questions? Call (310) 310-3704.

Which dogs benefit from physical therapy?

Dogs recovering from orthopedic surgery, such as TPLO or fracture repair, and dogs recovering from injuries benefit most. It helps restore mobility, reduce pain and rebuild strength, and it can also keep older dogs with stiff joints more comfortable.

What does a physical therapy session involve?

Sessions combine laser therapy, massage, therapeutic exercises and electrical stimulation as your dog needs them. We start with an assessment of the surgery or injury, mobility and pain, and adjust the plan as your dog gets stronger.

What is laser therapy for dogs?

Laser therapy applies low-level light to the affected area to reduce pain and inflammation and to accelerate tissue repair.

Does my dog need physical therapy after TPLO?

Physical therapy may be recommended during recovery from TPLO. Dr. Pedraza will tell you whether your dog would benefit and when to start.

TPLO recovery timeline →

How soon after surgery can physical therapy start?

It depends on the procedure and how your dog is healing. Dr. Pedraza will tell you when it is safe to begin, and until then it is important to follow the activity restrictions for your dog's surgery.

How many physical therapy sessions will my dog need?

It depends on your dog and on the surgery or injury. We set goals at the start, check progress as your dog gets stronger and adjust the plan, so you always know what comes next.

Can physical therapy help an older dog with arthritis?

It can help with comfort and mobility alongside other treatments. We begin with a pain assessment and build a plan that may include laser therapy, massage and therapeutic exercises, together with weight management and dental care when needed.

Will I be able to do exercises with my dog at home?

Yes. As your dog progresses we give you exercises to do at home, so the work between visits supports the progress made in each session.
