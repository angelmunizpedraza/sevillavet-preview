Pet Dentistry: Cleanings, Dental X-rays and Extractions

Most dental problems in dogs and cats are preventable. A yearly professional cleaning with full-mouth dental x-rays keeps your pet's mouth healthy and free of hidden pain.

At a glance

Why your pet's teeth matter

Periodontal disease is caused by bacteria on the teeth. The bacteria form plaque, plaque hardens into tartar, and tartar inflames the gums (gingivitis). Left untreated, the infection reaches the roots and the teeth loosen.

As the disease progresses it damages the bone around the teeth, and bacteria can enter the bloodstream and harm the kidneys, liver and heart. Weak teeth can also break on hard toys, causing infection and a lot of pain.

Recommended
A professional dental cleaning once a year
Includes
Oral exam, ultrasonic and hand scaling, and polishing
Dental x-rays
Full-mouth digital x-rays, recommended with every cleaning
Pain control
A nerve block lasting 6 to 8 hours before any extraction
Watch for
Bad breath, red or bleeding gums, broken or loose teeth, chewing on one side

How it develops

From plaque to periodontal disease

Dental pain usually builds slowly and pets learn to live with it, so most warning signs are easy to miss.

  • 01

    Plaque

    Bacteria build up on the surface of the teeth after every meal.

  • 02

    Tartar

    Plaque hardens into tartar that brushing alone can no longer remove.

  • 03

    Gingivitis

    The gums become red, swollen and may bleed. This stage is still reversible.

  • 04

    Periodontitis

    Infection reaches the roots and bone, teeth loosen and can damage other organs.

Professional cleaning

How a dental cleaning works

A dental cleaning for dogs and cats should include all the same steps as a cleaning for people.

  1. 1

    Exam and x-rays

    A thorough oral exam and full-mouth dental x-rays, interpreted by one of our doctors.
  2. 2

    Plan each tooth

    We decide which treatment, if any, is right for every individual tooth.
  3. 3

    Scale and polish

    Ultrasonic scaling, hand scaling and polishing remove plaque and tartar above and below the gum line.
  4. 4

    Treat

    Teeth with advanced periodontal disease may need periodontal surgery or extraction by one of our doctors.

Dental x-rays

Seeing what is under the gum line

A cleaning is not complete without full-mouth x-rays. The visible crown of a tooth can look perfectly normal while the root below is diseased. Once all the teeth are x-rayed, your veterinarian reviews them and decides what treatment each tooth needs.

We recommend adding full-mouth dental x-rays to every dental cleaning, so we stay ahead of problems hidden below the gums.

  • Early diagnosis of periodontal disease and problems below the gum line
  • Helps make sure your dog's or cat's mouth is pain-free
  • Higher image quality than traditional film x-rays
  • Instant results for a faster diagnosis
  • Less radiation than film x-rays
  • Digital radiography is better for the environment

Tooth extractions

When a tooth needs to come out

Pets rarely show dental pain. When a damaged tooth is removed, most owners notice a happier, more carefree pet.

Reasons for an extraction

  • Gum (periodontal) disease
  • Broken teeth
  • Unerupted teeth
  • Tooth decay
  • Root abscess

How we keep it comfortable

  • A nerve block that lasts 6 to 8 hours, given before any extraction
  • Simple extractions: the ligament is gently loosened until the tooth comes out
  • Complex extractions: the tooth is sectioned and each root removed separately
  • The socket is flushed and closed with absorbable sutures

Questions

Pet dentistry FAQ

Still have questions? Call (310) 310-3704.

How often should my dog's or cat's teeth be cleaned?

We recommend a professional dental cleaning once a year. Your veterinarian also checks your pet's teeth at every wellness exam and will tell you if a cleaning is needed sooner.

Why does my pet need dental x-rays?

A tooth can look normal above the gum line while the root is diseased. Full-mouth dental x-rays let us find and treat those problems, which is why we recommend them with every cleaning.

Is my pet asleep for a dental cleaning?

Yes. A thorough cleaning below the gum line and dental x-rays are done under general anesthesia, and your pet is monitored throughout the procedure.

Will an extraction be painful for my pet?

Before any extraction we give a nerve block that lasts six to eight hours to minimize pain and discomfort, and we send your pet home with medication if needed.

What are the signs of dental disease in dogs and cats?

Bad breath, red or bleeding gums, tartar on the teeth, broken or loose teeth, mouth ulcers and chewing on one side of the mouth. Many pets show no obvious signs at all.

How do I book a dental cleaning?

Call (310) 310-3704. If your pet has not been seen recently, we will start with an exam to plan the cleaning.
