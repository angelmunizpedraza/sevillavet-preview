A healthy start

Everything your new pet needs in the first year

A new puppy or kitten brings countless joyful moments. Amid all the excitement, their health care during the first year of life matters more than ever.

Our veterinarians create a healthcare plan tailored to your pet, and our team is here to answer questions and guide you through this important period, so you can enjoy every moment together.

Spaying and neutering

A gentler spay with laparoscopy

We offer laparoscopic (keyhole) spays: two tiny incisions instead of one large one, less pain and a recovery of usually 5 to 7 days, with no sutures to remove.

Because spaying is an elective procedure, we time it for your dog's health. For female dogs we usually recommend waiting until after the first heat cycle, typically 2 to 3 weeks after it has fully finished.

Questions

Puppy and kitten FAQ

Still have questions? Call (310) 310-3704.

When should I bring my new puppy or kitten to the vet?

Soon after you bring them home. The first visit lets us check that your pet is healthy, start or continue the vaccine series and plan the first year.

What vaccines do puppies and kittens need?

Puppies need the core vaccines against distemper, parvovirus, hepatitis and rabies; kittens need the core vaccines against feline distemper, calicivirus, rhinotracheitis and rabies. They are given as a series over several visits, and we add lifestyle vaccines when needed.

When should I spay my female dog?

We usually recommend waiting until after the first heat cycle, typically 2 to 3 weeks after it has fully finished. We will help you choose the right time for your dog.

Do you offer laparoscopic spays?

Yes. A laparoscopic spay uses two small incisions and a camera, so there is less pain, minimal scarring and a faster recovery, usually 5 to 7 days.

Can you help with training and socialization?

Yes. We share basic puppy training guidelines and tips to socialize your puppy or kitten with people and other animals, including the pets you already have.
